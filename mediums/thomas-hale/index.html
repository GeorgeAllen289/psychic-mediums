About Thomas Hale

Thomas Hale has worked as a healing medium for sixteen years and has held more than three and a half thousand sessions. He combines evidential impressions with what he calls a healing presence: a way of sitting with someone that is more about steadiness than about information. Clients often say they arrive tense and leave breathing differently, which is the effect he cares about most.

He began his career as a hospice volunteer, sitting with families through long nights. That work taught him how to be quiet beside someone in pain, and it still shapes how he practises. He is wary of dramatic statements and is more comfortable describing a sense of calm, a change in the atmosphere or a particular image that comes with warmth.

Thomas offers trauma-sensitive sessions. This means that he agrees in advance what subjects are off the table, checks in repeatedly, and lets you pause or stop without explanation. He does not ask you to relive anything. For people whose loss involved a sudden death, an illness or a difficult relationship, he feels that the pace of the sitting matters more than any single impression.

He also offers spiritual guidance for people trying to work out what they believe. He does not push any tradition and happily works with people who are religious, agnostic or somewhere in between. He is open about the fact that healing mediumship cannot cure physical or mental illness and is not a replacement for medical or psychological care. His articles on grief and rest are collected on his author page.

Thomas often ends a session by suggesting a simple practice for the following days: a short walk, a glass of water, ten minutes with no screens. He regards these as the part of the work that lasts. He also encourages people to talk with friends, family, a counsellor or a faith leader, and he is glad when a client tells him that a sitting opened a conversation they had been avoiding.

How a session with Thomas goes

  1. Agree the edges

    Thomas asks what you would like to avoid and what you hope for.

  2. Slow sitting

    He moves at your pace and checks in often.

  3. Rest

    The session ends with a few minutes of quiet and suggestions for the day.

Before you book

  • Decide in advance what subjects are too hard to discuss.
  • Wear something comfortable.
  • Have a quiet, undemanding hour afterwards.

Questions to bring

  • My loss was sudden. Can the session be paced around that?
  • How do you check that I am okay during a sitting?
  • I am not religious. Is that a problem?

Mediumship is a spiritual practice, not a science, and no medium can guarantee contact with a particular person. Sessions are offered for comfort and reflection, are not a substitute for medical, legal, financial or mental-health care, and are for adults aged 18 and over. Thomas — frequently asked questions

Can healing mediumship treat illness?

No. It can offer calm and comfort. It is not medical treatment.

Can I stop at any time?

Yes, without needing to give a reason.
